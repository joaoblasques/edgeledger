Why this page has no numbers yet

The forecast log is filling up, but a forecast cannot be scored until the market it refers to settles. Most tracked contracts resolve months out, so there is a deliberate lag between "we are logging predictions" and "we can tell you whether they were any good".

The scoreboard below is published empty on purpose. The metrics were chosen and committed to in advance — before any of them could be computed — rather than selected afterwards from whichever ones happened to flatter the result. That ordering is the entire point, and because every row is timestamped and hash-chained, it is checkable rather than merely claimed.

The current model is market_mirror: it forecasts exactly the market's own mid price, so it has zero edge by construction. It is not trying to be good. It is a canary — if its Brier score turns out to be distinguishable from the market's, the measurement pipeline is broken, not the model. Real models arrive from month 5.

Verifying the record

Every forecast row is hash-chained to the row before it, and the log itself is committed to the public repository. The current chain head is published at docs/chain-head.json and republished on every run.

Anyone can check the whole history, with no credentials and no access to anything of mine:

git clone https://github.com/joaoblasques/edgeledger
cd edgeledger && uv sync
uv run python3 -c "from pathlib import Path; from edgeledger.forecast.log import read_rows, verify_chain, head_hash; rows = read_rows(Path('data')); verify_chain(rows); print(len(rows), head_hash(Path('data')))"

Compare the hash it prints against docs/chain-head.json. If a single forecast had been edited, deleted, or back-dated after the fact, the recomputed chain would not match — and the mismatch would name the exact row. Corrections, when they are needed, are new rows carrying supersedes_forecast_id; nothing is ever overwritten.

That is what turns a track record from a claim into something you can check for yourself.
