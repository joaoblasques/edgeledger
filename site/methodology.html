Methodology

How a forecast is written, and how it gets scored.

Overview Methodology Results Learning

This page mirrors docs/methodology.md in the repository, which is the source of truth and is updated whenever scoring logic changes.

What gets forecast

Every tracked open market, on both venues, gets a p_hat — the probability of the "yes" outcome — from whichever model is currently in production for that market category. Month 1 runs exactly two deliberately naive models; real models arrive from month 5.

How a forecast is written

  1. A model computes p_hat from a feature_vector built entirely from bronze data captured at or before feature_cutoff_ts_utc.
  2. The market's own state (bid, ask, mid, spread, depth, volume) is read at that same moment and embedded in the row. It is never joined in afterwards.
  3. The row is hash-chained to the previous row and appended. It is never edited again.

The naive baselines (month 1)

market_mirror v1.0.0 — p_hat = mkt_yes_mid at T-24h before close. Zero edge by construction. It exists to validate the pipeline end to end: if market_mirror's Brier score isn't statistically indistinguishable from brier_market, something in the pipeline is broken, not the model.

base_rate v1.0.0 — p_hat is the historical frequency of the outcome class. Genuinely naive, and expected to lose to the market. The point is to have an honest floor.

Every later model, from month 5 onward, is judged against both.

Scoring

Scoring happens downstream, in the recomputable forecast_scored view, joining forecast_log against resolutions and closing prices at query time. The forecast row itself never learns the outcome.

Metrics are always reported as a pair — model against market baseline:

MetricWhat it measures
Brier score (brier vs brier_market) Mean squared error of the probability forecast.
Log loss Penalizes confident wrong calls more heavily than Brier does.
Calibration Do forecasts of "70%" resolve yes about 70% of the time, with a bootstrap confidence interval.
Closing-line value (CLV) The market's own mid at forecast time minus its mid at close. The metric a desk trusts most, because it isolates timing skill from luck.

No single-number "accuracy" is ever reported on its own. It is meaningless without the market baseline alongside it.

What's out of scope so far

No real forecasting model, no sizing or Kelly logic, and no paper execution exists yet. This page grows a section per model version as they ship — Elo and Bradley-Terry from month 3, logistic regression from month 5, Poisson and Dixon-Coles from month 7.
